# Elektor 2010-7_8 - not a standalone puzzle
# "Hexadocube" (p. 60-61), designer Claude Ghyselen: six hexadoku grids on
# the faces of a cube, printed unfolded across the double page - what the
# article calls the development. The magazine states that solving a single
# grid on its own is impossible.
#
# The net, measured off the spread (each box is one 16x16 face):
#
#                             +----+
#                             | 4  |     the four grids of the middle row
#    +----+----+----+----+----+----+     wrap around the cube, face 4 is
#    | 0  | 1  | 2  | 3  |               its top and face 5 its bottom
#    +----+----+----+----+----+
#                             | 5  |
#                             +----+
#
# Faces 0 and 1 are on p. 60, faces 2, 3, 4 and 5 on p. 61; the strip
# crosses the gutter, so the puzzle is one figure over two pages.
#
# The link is the cells - "cases" in the designer's French - along each
# cube edge, coloured pale yellow and left empty in the opening situation:
# they are one cell seen from two faces and must hold the same value on
# both. The eight cube corners, coloured orange, belong to three faces at
# once. That leaves 6*256 - 12*16 + 8 = 1352 cells in 6*48 - 12 = 276
# units, because a boundary line of one face *is* a line of its
# neighbour. The 84-clue mask in 2010-7_8_mask.txt is one face out of the
# six and is meaningless on its own.
#
# The bright cells inside a face are not part of the rules: they are the
# clues that spell out the face number in the background, "arranged in
# such a way that the numbers on opposite faces always add up to 7, just
# like on a die".
#
# The solver handles this layout since the hexadocube variant was added
# (see cube.go); the six faces are folded onto the surface lattice of a
# cube and the edge identifications fall out of the coordinates. Use
#
#     hexadoku.exe -gen hexadocube
#
# for a generated one. What is still missing here is only the reading:
# the clues are a graphic on a scan, so all six faces have to be
# transcribed visually, and there is no printed solution grid to check
# them against - the uniqueness of the whole cube would have to do it.
