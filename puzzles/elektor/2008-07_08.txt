# Elektor 2008-07_08 - not a standalone puzzle
# "AlphaSudoku" (p. 118): the double issue prints this instead of a
# hexadoku; p. 119 only announces the winners of the previous one (the
# May 2008 code is 36815).
#
# 25x25 cells over 25 symbols: the numbers 1 through 9 and the letters A
# through P - "including the letter 'O', hence the absence of the '0' in
# the numbers", which is how a 25-symbol alphabet stays readable. Boxes
# are 5x5, marked by a coloured outline.
#
# It carries an extra rule of its own, and it is the ancestor of the
# later ones: "The puzzle is essentially a (16x16) Alphadoku comprising
# nine classic (9x9) Sudokus using numbers 1 through 9." Nine 9x9 blocks
# inside the 25x25 grid are shaded and must additionally be valid
# sudokus over the digits alone - so those 81 cells carry two alphabets
# at once, 25 values by the outer rules and 9 by the inner ones.
#
# Claude Ghyselen names it as his own first puzzle for Elektor in the
# 2012 article ("one of my suggestions used in the 2008 annual double
# edition: the Alphasudoku"), ahead of the Hexamurai 7-8/2009, the
# Hexadocube 7-8/2010 and the EUC Penta-Hexadoku 7-8/2012.
#
# Out of reach here for the same reason as the Alphanumski of 7-8/2007:
# 25 values do not fit a solver built around 9 or 16.
