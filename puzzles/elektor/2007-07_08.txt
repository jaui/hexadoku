# Elektor 2007-07_08 - not a standalone puzzle
# "Alphanumski" (p. 140-141), creator Gery Szcepanski - the one special
# puzzle of the series that is not by Claude Ghyselen.
#
# It is not a hexadoku and not a verbund of overlapping grids either: it
# is one single 36x36 grid, printed across the double page, 18 columns on
# each side. The article states the rules outright - "all letters of the
# alphabet (A through Z) and all numerals 0 through 9 should occur only
# once in every line (1x36), every column (1x36) and every box (6x6;
# identified by red outlines and a background colour)". The boxes are
# shaded in a chequerboard of 6x6 blocks, which is decoration and not a
# rule, unlike the chequerboard of the 2012 penta-hexadoku.
#
# So the geometry is the plainest of the whole series - rows, columns,
# boxes of one grid - and what puts it out of reach here is the alphabet:
# this solver carries candidates as uint16 bitmasks and is built for 9 or
# 16 values, while Alphanumski needs 36. Supporting it means widening the
# value representation everywhere, not adding a layout.
#
# The 160-clue mask in 2007-07_08_mask.txt is a 16x16 window the grid
# detector cut out of that much larger lattice and is meaningless on its
# own.
#
# The article also names the predecessor: an "Alphadoku" of 25x25 cells
# in the double issue 7-8/2006, which this archive does not reach back to.
