# "Alphanumski", by Gery Szcepanski - the one special puzzle of the
# series that is not by Claude Ghyselen. One single 36x36 grid printed
# across the double page, 18 columns on each side, so the two halves are
# fitted separately and joined left to right.
#
# The article states the rules outright - "all letters of the alphabet (A
# through Z) and all numerals 0 through 9 should occur only once in every
# line (1x36), every column (1x36) and every box (6x6; identified by red
# outlines and a background colour)". The boxes are shaded in a
# chequerboard of 6x6 blocks, which is decoration and not a rule, unlike
# the chequerboard of the 2012 penta-hexadoku. So the geometry is the
# plainest of the whole series, and only the 36-value alphabet puts it
# outside the ordinary solver.
#
# As printed the puzzle has TWO solutions. They differ in exactly four
# cells - r26c4, r26c33, r29c4 and r29c33 - which form a rectangle whose
# two values 9 and X can be swapped around it. All four are blank on the
# page (checked against the render at 300 dpi), so this is a flaw of the
# puzzle and not of the reading: 820 of the 824 glyphs on the two pages
# are clues, and the remaining four sit in the running text.
#
# It never mattered to a reader. None of the four cells is one of the
# seven grey answer cells, so both solutions give the same code, and
# Elektor announced it in 10/2007: "The solution of the july/august 2007
# puzzle is: LVC4ZM1." The reconstruction reproduces it.
#
# Two side files survive from before the puzzle was read: the 160-clue
# 2007-07_08_mask.txt and 2007-07_08_ocr.txt are a 16x16 window the grid
# detector cut out of this much larger lattice, and are meaningless.
#
# The article also names the predecessor: an "Alphadoku" of 25x25 cells
# in the double issue 7-8/2006. That one is in the archive after all, from
# the Dutch edition - see 2006-07_08.txt, where it is called Alfadoku.
